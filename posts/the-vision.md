**It Started With an Idea**

The vision for the Double Cab actually existed before I even bought the vehicle.

Shortly before purchasing it, my cousin and I were talking about my plans to eventually travel around Africa. We were discussing what kind of vehicle would make sense for such an adventure when he mentioned that he had a T4 Double Cab available for a relatively small amount of money. And he would sell it to me. Around the same time, we stumbled across a YouTube video showing a rather interesting T3 Double Cab conversion.
Someone had built a raised platform over the pickup bed, essentially creating a rooftop terrace, with a rooftop tent on top. The idea immediately caught my attention.

I remember thinking: That's it. That's exactly what I want to build.

The T3 in the video was already a Syncro, while the T4 I was looking at still had front-wheel drive. But that didn't stop the idea from taking shape.
A Double Cab with a rooftop terrace, a tent on top and enough space underneath for everything needed on a longer journey. Something different from the usual campervan setup.
And, admittedly, something that simply looked pretty cool.

**Why a Double Cab?**

There are plenty of perfectly good Volkswagen T4 campers out there. Standard vans and Caravelles are practical, comfortable and offer plenty of possibilities for conversion.
But I wanted something different.
A Double Cab is a relatively unusual starting point, especially when it comes to building an expedition vehicle. It combines a proper passenger cabin with an open loading area, offering a completely different set of possibilities.
The more I thought about it, the more sense the concept made.
With a rooftop tent, the sleeping area would be completely separate from the interior. That would leave the space inside the vehicle available for cooking, storage and living.
The pickup bed could become an outdoor living area, a kitchen extension or simply additional cargo space.
And who knows – perhaps even enough room to bring a motorcycle along.
Having travelled through Norway with a rooftop tent before, I already knew that I enjoyed this way of travelling. Sleeping above the ground, waking up with a view and having the rest of the vehicle available for everything else just works particularly well for me.
The Double Cab seemed like the perfect platform to take that concept a little further.

**More Than Just a Camper**

I didn't want to build a conventional campervan with a bed, a small kitchen and a few storage compartments.
The idea was to create something closer to a compact expedition vehicle.
Something capable of handling rough roads, remote tracks and the occasional difficult passage. Not necessarily an extreme off-road machine, but a vehicle that wouldn't have to turn around whenever the tarmac disappeared.
**That meant the original front-wheel-drive setup was going to be a problem.**
If I really wanted to explore more remote areas, having four driven wheels would make a considerable difference. A Syncro drivetrain, ideally with a rear differential lock, quickly became part of the vision.

Ground clearance was another important factor.
The vehicle would need a suitable suspension setup with stronger springs and shock absorbers, together with a suspension lift and larger all-terrain tyres.

It wasn't just about making the truck look more adventurous. The suspension and drivetrain would have to cope with additional weight, rough surfaces and long distances.
That also meant thinking about reinforced bearings, bushes and other components that would need to withstand the additional demands.

Essentially, the entire vehicle would have to be reconsidered.

**A Little Kitchen on Wheels**

The interior concept was relatively straightforward.
Behind the driver's cabin, I wanted to build a compact kitchen setup that could be pulled out towards the side door.
The idea was to keep things simple and practical, without permanently filling the available space with furniture.
A refrigerator, an induction cooktop and enough room to prepare a proper meal would be the essentials.
Ideally, the kitchen could slide out when needed, making it possible to cook from outside the vehicle while keeping the interior accessible.

The Double Cab offered an interesting advantage here: I could use the space behind the front seats for the main living and storage area while keeping the pickup bed available for additional equipment.
And with the rooftop tent providing the sleeping accommodation, there would be no need to sacrifice valuable interior space for a permanent bed.

**Energy Independence**

Another important part of the concept was being able to stay independent for extended periods.
If you're travelling through remote areas, constantly looking for campsites with electricity or somewhere to recharge your equipment isn't exactly ideal.
Solar power therefore became an essential part of the plan.
My initial idea was to mount solar panels either above the rooftop terrace or directly on the rooftop tent, depending on how the final setup would work out.
Combined with a sufficiently large battery and a proper charging system, this would allow me to power the refrigerator, cooking equipment and other electrical devices without having to rely on external power sources.
The goal wasn't necessarily to be completely independent forever, but to have enough energy available to spend several days away from civilisation without constantly worrying about the battery level.

**The Plan Was Taking Shape**

By this point, the basic concept was becoming fairly clear.

A Volkswagen T4 Double Cab with:

- Syncro all-wheel drive and a rear differential lock.
- A reinforced, lifted suspension setup.
- Larger all-terrain tyres.
- A rooftop terrace with a rooftop tent.
- A compact, flexible kitchen setup.
- Solar power and a substantial auxiliary battery.
- Enough storage space for equipment and potentially even a motorcycle.

In other words, a small expedition vehicle built around a rather unusual platform.
Of course, there was one small detail I hadn't fully considered when dreaming up all these modifications:
The truck itself was still in pretty rough shape.
Before any of these ideas could become reality, there was a considerable amount of restoration work waiting for me. And converting a front-wheel-drive T4 into a Syncro was going to be a project of its own.
But at least I now knew what I was working towards.
I had a vehicle, a vision and a growing list of things to figure out.
And sometimes, that's all it takes to get a project started.

**Now it was time to turn the ideas into reality.**
