Sometimes, a new chapter begins long before you know exactly what it will look like.

After spending three years living in Norway, I had started planning a return journey. I wanted to get back on the road, explore new places, enjoy the freedom of travelling independently and spend more time outdoors. One thing, however, was still missing: the right vehicle.

For quite some time, I had been thinking about getting an older vehicle and turning it into a camper myself. Not a ready-made motorhome, but a project of my own. Something with character, a bit of history and plenty of room for ideas.

At the end of December 2025, that idea finally became reality. I bought a 1995 Volkswagen T4 Double Cab Pickup and drove it all the way from Stuttgart back to Chemnitz. And just like that, a new chapter began – both for me and for a vehicle that had already seen quite a bit of life.

**Not Your Typical VW Bus**

My new project is a Volkswagen T4 Double Cab with a flatbed. And that's exactly what made this particular version so appealing to me.

<figure class="story-inline story-inline-right"><img src="https://86ec824d1amijkwd.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/blog-inline/1794526115887-IMG_3269-GF38cBa23eyWXUUhwIpNJywC0srUOM.jpeg" alt="Reisefoto" loading="lazy" /></figure>



Classic VW camper vans and panel vans are still relatively common. The Double Cab Pickup, on the other hand, is a much less familiar sight. It has a distinctly utilitarian character and feels much more like a traditional work vehicle than the typical VW camper.

That's precisely what I liked about it. The combination of a double cab and an open loading area offers completely different possibilities compared to a conventional van. I could immediately imagine turning it into something special – a vehicle that would eventually serve not only as a practical everyday companion but also as a unique travel and expedition vehicle.

Under the bonnet, things are relatively straightforward. My T4 is powered by the 2.4-litre naturally aspirated diesel engine, known by its engine code AAB. It's an inline five-cylinder producing 78 horsepower, with front-wheel drive.
<figure class="story-inline story-inline-right"><img src="https://86ec824d1amijkwd.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/blog-inline/1794525910519-IMG_2749-N5LOE9swKbLk8514KGbqyrsDao5A5x.jpeg" alt="Reisefoto" loading="lazy" /></figure>


It's certainly not a fast vehicle, but the engine is known for its relatively simple and robust construction. Compared to modern vehicles, there's very little electronics involved and no complicated engine management system.

And that was an important factor for me.

**A Vehicle with a History**

One of the things I find particularly fascinating about older vehicles isn't just their technology or design. It's the history they carry with them.

My T4 originally served as a company vehicle for a water utility in southern Germany. Later, it was taken over by one of the employees, who mainly used it for transporting firewood.

At some point, it changed hands again and ended up with a group of young people who took it on the so-called Carbage Run. It's a rather unusual rally in which participants travel through different countries in old, cheap and often pretty rough-looking vehicles. My T4 was taken through Bosnia and Serbia as part of this adventure.

So, the vehicle had already experienced quite a few different chapters before it eventually found its way to me.

And that's exactly what I like about it. It's not a blank canvas or a freshly restored classic that's only supposed to look good. It has character, signs of use and a story of its own.

I really like the idea of giving this vehicle another life. Another story, another chapter – hopefully filled with plenty of journeys and adventures.

**Why This T4?**

I'm not a trained mechanic, and I haven't had much experience working on vehicles so far. Nevertheless, I've wanted to get more involved in mechanics and vehicle maintenance for quite some time.

The T4 seemed like a good starting point. Its technology is relatively straightforward, the engine doesn't rely on much electronics and, in principle, there's still a lot you can work on yourself.

I'd like to learn how to repair things, work on the vehicle, weld and solve problems on my own. Of course, it won't always be easy, and I'm sure I'll make a few mistakes along the way. But that's part of the whole experience.

I deliberately didn't want to buy a finished vehicle where everything had already been taken care of. What really appeals to me is the idea of building it up step by step, doing as much as possible myself and gradually learning more about mechanics and vehicle conversions.

The Vision: A Small Off-Road Expedition Vehicle

From the very beginning, the idea was to turn this Double Cab into a unique camper and off-road expedition vehicle. 

Something I could travel in independently, sleep outdoors and take beyond the usual tourist routes. I had actually bought the rooftop tent before purchasing the T4, so all I needed was the right vehicle to go with my plans.

I'm imagining a simple, functional conversion. Nothing luxurious, just a reliable companion that offers as much freedom as possible. A vehicle that can be repaired, modified and improved, even after long journeys.

There's still quite a bit of work ahead before it gets anywhere near that vision. But that's all part of the project. I'm looking forward to getting to know the vehicle, coming up with my own solutions and gradually turning this old workhorse into my personal expedition vehicle.

I'll share more about the condition I actually bought the T4 in and what I discovered once I started looking closer in one of the upcoming posts. Because the real work on this project was only just about to begin.

One thing is certain: this isn't just an old vehicle. It's the beginning of a new chapter – and I'm excited to see where this T4 will take me.
