On March 31, 2026, it was time for me to say goodbye to Scandinavia. My journey started in Trondheim, Norway. Ahead of me lay roughly 1,400 kilometers from Lillehammer to Chemnitz. But instead of getting it over with as fast as possible, I had a different plan.
I wanted to take my time. Enjoy the landscape one more time, stop where it felt right, sleep outside, do a bit of off-roading, and really take in those last moments in Scandinavia.
With me was my faithful Seat Alhambra. A real workhorse with around 400,000 kilometers on the clock at the time. And I had a little challenge in mind: Could I actually make it from the Norwegian border all the way home on just one tank of fuel?
At the border, somewhere around Elverum, I filled the 70-liter tank right up. After that, it had to last – at least that was the plan.

Four Days Toward Home
Day 1 – From Trondheim to Lillehammer
The first leg took me from Trondheim to Lillehammer. One more drive through the Norwegian landscape, taking it all in, stopping wherever I felt like it. I had no reason to rush. The journey itself was the destination, one last time.
Day 2 – From Lillehammer to Karlstad
On the second day, I continued into Sweden, all the way to Karlstad. I was especially taken with the area around Lake Vänern near Karlstad. I spent a lot of time outside there, went fishing, and was able to really soak up that Scandinavian feeling of life once more.
I met some lovely people and caught some really nice perch. Those are exactly the kind of unplanned moments that make a road trip for me: In the morning you don't necessarily know what the day will bring – and that's  precisely why it stays in your memory.

Day 3 – From Karlstad to Trelleborg
The journey continued south to Trelleborg. Along the way, I took in everything that made these last days in Scandinavia so special: Northern lights, nights outside, little adventures off the beaten track, and many impressions I didn't just want to leave behind.
From Trelleborg, I was then to take the ferry to Rostock.

Day 4 – From Rostock to Chemnitz
On the fourth day, only the final leg was left. From Rostock back to Chemnitz. Home wasn't far anymore – and the tension slowly grew: Would that one tank of fuel really last all the way to the finish?
And indeed: Yes!
I arrived in Chemnitz with, according to the display, about ten kilometers of range left. Done. From the Norwegian border to home on a single tank of fuel. And that with an Alhambra that already had around 400,000 kilometers under its belt.
Four Nights on the Road
During the trip, I slept exclusively in the roof tent or directly in the car. Most nights were really beautiful and were just as much a part of the adventure as the driving itself.
Only the last night in Trelleborg wasn't exactly a highlight. Sleeping in the car right at the ferry terminal was more of a practical solution than a cozy place to spend the night. But that's part of it too. Not every night on a road trip is a postcard night.
A Farewell That Felt Like a Little Adventure
Looking back, this drive was much more than just a journey home. It was my personal little farewell to Scandinavia. Fishing once more, sleeping outside, experiencing the northern lights, meeting new people, and just setting off without planning every moment.
Four days, four nights, many impressions, and a faithful companion who once again didn't let me down.
Sometimes it's not the big expeditions that stay with you most. Sometimes it's the last drive home – when you consciously decide to enjoy the detour.
And sometimes, a single tank of fuel and ten kilometers of range left are enough for that.
